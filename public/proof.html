01Discrimination — "it finds the 3% that will bounce."
0.913 ROC-AUC out-of-fold
Across 112,713 held-out predictions the model ranks a debit that will dishonour above one that won't about 91 times in 100. On a rare 3%-event the honest metric is PR-AUC: 0.46 vs 0.03 for a coin flip — 15.7× better at surfacing the few debits worth acting on.

02Falsification — "we deleted the signal and the model died."
0.913 → 0.501 AUC
We regenerated the world with the causal interactions switched off — failures become base-rate coin flips, independent of amount and timing — then retrained and re-evaluated the identical pipeline on it. A model that was leaking or memorising labels would still score high. Ours falls to 0.50, a coin flip, and stops beating the payday heuristic. That collapse is the strongest evidence the 0.913 is real learned signal, not a harness artifact. We ran this ablation on ourselves.

03Impact — "what it's worth, in recovered dollars."
46% recovered on the first retry
Take the 3,334 held-out NSF failures ($550,657 at risk) and give each one retry. Naive next-day recovers 14.5%; a strong payday+2 heuristic 38.0%; the model 46.0% — an extra $57,900 over the best heuristic, closing 55% of the ceiling a perfect oracle could ever reach.

04Depth — "it rediscovered a variable we never gave it."
0 — the pay-cycle was never a feature
Each payer's pay-cycle is a hidden latent the model never sees. From debit history alone it builds a risk curve that peaks in the low-balance trough just before payday and collapses after — and it does so separately and correctly for 7-, 14- and 30-day payers. You can't fake that by memorising labels; the model had to reconstruct the mechanism.

Caveat: the true cycle is used only to split the plot into panels, never as a model input — auditable in generator.py, where latents carry a leading underscore and are excluded from FEATURES. Per-point values are the rendered curve, not in metrics.json.
05Calibration — "the probabilities mean what they say."
0.0198 Brier score
A predicted 20% risk really does fail about 20% of the time — the reliability curve hugs the diagonal. That calibration is what lets us set an honest intervention threshold instead of guessing; the Brier is 31% better than a forecaster that just predicts the base rate every time (skill score 0.31).

06Provenance — "synthetic, disclosed, and calibrated to real BECS."
3.1% dishonour · 95% NSF
We say it plainly: the ledger is synthetic. But it's calibrated to published AU BECS base rates and — the part that matters — the pay-cycle is a hidden latent, so the model earns its score by reconstructing structure, not reading a planted answer.
