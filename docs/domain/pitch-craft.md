# Pitch craft — what wins a 2–3 minute demo night

> Research briefing (sourced, July 2026). Part of the domain-mastery asset.

## The structures that win (with timing)

**The YC canon.** YC's official guide prescribes: crisp what-we-do intro → problem → product+customer → bottom-up opportunity → traction → team (only truly notable credentials) → a close that re-lands 3–4 "vertebrae" — the only points the room will retain. Two hard delivery laws: **≤7 words per slide** and "speak unnaturally slowly." YC's actual stage format is ~2:15 with a **hard mic cut at 135 seconds**; the working rule is *rehearse to 120* — a 10–15% time buffer under the ceiling.

**The 8-line skeleton (from a study of 87 YC pitches).** Every great pitch fits 8 lines: **What → Problem → Failure (why current solutions break) → Solution (mechanism, one sentence) → Why Now → Proof → Team → Vision.** The winning variant is **numbers-first**: 67% of successful pitches open with proof, not story. Crisp, specific hooks got ~3x more follow-up meetings than vision statements; a specific 12-word company description outperforms a vague 22-word one. Ruthless rule: *if a sentence adds no new information, delete it.*

**The one-number rule.** Investors remember exactly **one number per company**. Lead with a single metric paired with growth-rate and duration ("$40K MRR, +30% w/w, 3 months"); putting two numbers on one slide means the room keeps neither. Every other figure in the pitch must be spoken flat, not punched.

**Techstars.** Four sections — **Intro / Demo / Biz Stuff / Landing**. The intro (30–60s) must do three jobs: who you are, why you're credible, why the problem matters — and their coaching is to "spend 75% of your prep getting this intro right," because attention lost in the first 30 seconds is unrecoverable. The **Landing** is a 30-second close that *asks for something specific* — never end on "thank you."

**Hackathon-specific (David Beckett, the 3-minute-pitch coach).** Structure: Problem → Solution → What's Unique → Implementation/Viability → what's in it for each stakeholder. **Read the judging criteria first and map a beat to each one.** Budget **~400 words for 3 minutes** (~133 wpm) — this is the single most violated rule. Power of three: exactly three takeaways, restated at the close. "It's just not professional to overrun the time."

## Delivery technique

- **Pace:** 130–150 wpm, i.e., markedly slower than conversation. Vary it — speed for excitement, slow for the money lines. **Silence is a weapon**: a 2-beat pause after the key number forces the room to process it.
- **Slide-to-speech ratio:** slides are billboards, not scripts. Techstars: "people will listen or read — they won't do both." Flowjam's redundancy test: **if your narration repeats the slide, delete one of them.** 60-pt-legible from the back row, one idea per slide, black-on-white, no cumulative graphs (YC calls them "always suspicious"), no bullet lists.
- **Body:** weight even, no pacing, face the room not the screen. Fatal errors: jargon, exaggeration, apologising for slides, reading verbatim, opening with "hi, my name is."

## Demo choreography

- **The near-universal rule is: never type live.** Pre-recorded 10–15s clips beat live demos; judges "have seen things crash — they'll respect a smooth backup more than a painful live recovery." Flowjam lists live demos among the 7 critical mistakes.
- **The exception that wins hackathons:** ONE live moment, if it's the proof-of-realness the judges specifically doubt — but it must be a rehearsed golden path with a pre-recorded fallback, and the switch itself rehearsed (pre-agreed tripwire, a scripted line, <5s to video, no apology).
- **The gasp beat (Jobs doctrine):** every great presentation has exactly **one** moment people repeat the next day — identified in advance, built up to, tightly scripted, relentlessly rehearsed. Two competing wow-moments cancel each other. Jobs framed demos from the customer's shoes: "Now what if I wanted to do X? Here's what that looks like."
- **Backup discipline:** record the fallback video the first day the live path goes green; be able to deliver the whole pitch with no projector at all.

## Q&A judo

- **Answer-first:** direct answer in one sentence, one supporting fact, stop. Trailing on reads as nerves.
- **Steel-manning:** restate a hostile question in a form *stronger* than the asker used, then answer it. It signals you've thought harder about the weakness than they have.
- **The pivot play (for real weaknesses):** Acknowledge → Bridge ("the way we've addressed it is…") → Reframe to the adjacent strength. Answer the substance, never the aggression; some panellists are hostile on purpose to test composure.
- **Never bluff.** "I don't know — here's how we'll find out by X" beats fabrication everywhere, and doubly in Australia (below). Prep = write the top 5–10 expected questions, run round-table "spitfire" mock Q&A with the team.

## Rehearsal protocol

Consensus across sources: **script it in full → cut by one-third → 50+ full spoken run-throughs**, with founders at the top end reporting 100–200. Ash Rust's demo-day regimen: **10 run-throughs per day in the final week (~70 total), filming every one on your phone and watching it back** — self-review is the fastest tightening loop. Rehearse to 85–90% of the time limit. Do at least one run on the actual venue AV, and one with no slides at all.

## Australian specifics

- **Tall-poppy dynamics are real and measured:** 66% of AU startup leaders say tall poppy syndrome is alive in the ecosystem; 80% have experienced it. AU founders systematically under-claim relative to US peers ("quiet confidence"), and the audience punishes swagger-without-receipts. The winning register: **brag with numbers and artifacts, never adjectives** — let a sourced figure or a live line-item do the boasting, and aim any aggression at incumbents, not at yourself or the host.
- **AU investors are traction-and-honesty biased:** Australian angels/VCs are described as "straightforward, data-driven, and sceptical of hype"; founders who *acknowledge challenges honestly* build more trust than polished-but-vague optimism. Disclosure-as-strength is on-culture here in a way it isn't at a US demo day.
- **This exact venue has a format precedent:** The Founders Union (Scalare Partners, ASX:SCP, CEO Carolyn Breeze) ran its Sydney "Investor Ready Pitch Night" at Tank Stream Labs with six startups doing **two-minute presentations to a judge panel** — so plan the Cadence pitch to survive a 2:00 compression, not just fill 3:00. The Pinch hackathon ("Pinch Me! I Want 50K", Pinch Payments + TFU, $50K) is explicitly a founder-to-capital and API-adoption funnel: the pitch's true conversion goal is *the meeting after* — same as YC, where the 2-minute pitch is optimised for one thing: making the room want a follow-up, not closing the deal on stage.
