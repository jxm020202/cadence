# The Senior-in-30-Days Playbook

> Synthesis of the four domain briefings. THE career-asset document.

## The 20 facts to know cold

1. BECS scale: 3.5 billion payments worth $17.4 trillion in 2024 — roughly 90% of Australian retail account-to-account value (RBA Risk Assessment). This is the number that establishes the rail is not dying, it IS the system.
2. The BECS clock: six file exchanges per business day (10:00, 13:00, 16:00, 18:30, 20:45, 22:30 Sydney time); the first five settle same-day in RITS (since November 2013), the 22:30 file settles at 9am next morning. Knowing the exchange times cold is the single strongest operator tell.
3. Direct debits have no positive confirmation — success is the ABSENCE of a dishonour. Return codes (2 = payment stopped, 3 = account closed, 5 = no/incorrect account, 6 = refer to customer) land next business day, up to three. Code 6 is the insufficient-funds workhorse and the one Cadence predicts.
4. The DDR mandate is held by the business, not the bank, and varying drawing arrangements requires at least 14 days' written notice. This is why mandate hygiene is a merchant-side software problem.
5. BECS Procedures v E071 (effective 1 September 2025) tightened DDR evidence: explicit T&Cs for DDR Service Agreements, sponsor-bank approval, enhanced minimum standards, stronger sanctions/AML screening — a fresh compliance burden every debit user now carries.
6. AusPayNet set a June 2030 BECS end-date in November 2023 and formally REMOVED it in December 2025 — Chair John Brogden said the majority of members no longer believe 2030 is achievable. The decommissioning failed; cite the reversal, not the target.
7. RBA March 2026 Risk Assessment: 'insufficient consensus' on bulk payments and a threat of 'further action... in the public interest' if coordination stalls; draft A2A vision April 2026, final roadmap due later in 2026. The regulator is now governing by deadline-threat.
8. NPP 2025: ~2 billion real-time payments (~155m/month, ~$7bn/day), 128 million reachable accounts, 25+ million PayIDs registered. The rail works; adoption of PayTo on top of it is what lags.
9. PayTo launched June 2022 — and three major banks missed go-live, only committing to April 2023 after RBA Governor letters. Bank foot-dragging is documented history, not opinion.
10. PayTo adoption reality: 90%+ of NPP-enabled personal accounts are eligible but only ~5% of recurring payment volume had converted by end-2025; PayTo agreement value was ~0.1% of BECS direct-debit value; RBA found growth comes from NEW agreements, not migration of existing debits; mandate porting was pulled from the AP+ roadmap.
11. Where PayTo does work it is excellent: Zepto PayTo Index (Nov 2025) — e-commerce PayTo +301% in six months, 98.9% settlement rate, 4.94s median settlement; one bank lifted mandate authorisation conversion from 40% to 70% purely via real-time push notifications. The bottleneck is bank authorisation UX.
12. 1 October 2026 double-hit: surcharging banned on eftpos/Mastercard/Visa debit, prepaid and credit (consumers currently pay ~$1.6bn of $1.8bn total annual surcharges), and interchange cut — debit 8c→6c / 0.20%→0.12%, consumer credit 0.50%→0.30% — worth ~$910m/yr to merchants. Price A2A against the POST-October blended card rate.
13. Least-cost routing: 84% of merchants enabled in-person by December 2025 (up from 50% in June 2022); online LCR available to 97% but enablement lags; no formal mandate — only RBA 'expectations' plus 6-monthly reporting. An RBA 'expectation' is a pre-mandate.
14. Payments System Modernisation Bill received Royal Assent 19 September 2025; Tranche 1 exposure draft (12 March 2026) brings PSPs into AFSL licensing and APRA supervision for SVFs >$200m. Payment-initiation/facilitation software is likely inside the perimeter — have a licensing one-liner ready.
15. Payday Super takes effect 1 July 2026 with the NPP as the real-time rail for super contributions — a forced-march driver of corporate NPP adoption regardless of PayTo sentiment.
16. The regulator map, recited without hesitation: RBA/PSB = system policy and designation; ASIC = conduct/licensing + ePayments Code; APRA = prudential (major SVFs); AUSTRAC = AML/CTF; ACCC = competition; AusPayNet = self-regulatory BECS rulebook; AP+ = scheme operator (the ACCC-authorised 9 Sept 2021 amalgamation of BPAY, eftpos and NPPA under a court-enforceable undertaking).
17. The consolidation ledger — every independent recurring-payments asset bought in ~24 months: Pinch→Fiserv (completed 4 April 2025; Pinch + Money Money = US$365M net, powering Clover/PayFac APAC via Glassbox), Ezypay→Xplor (closing Dec 2025), Nuapay→GoCardless (€32.75M, Sept 2024), Pin Payments + SecurePay + Adatree→Fat Zebra, and Payrix→FIS→Global Payments — which is buying Worldpay for US$24.25B while divesting Issuer Solutions to FIS for US$13.5B, putting Ezidebit and Payrix under one owner.
18. The direct-debit old guard: Ezidebit (Brisbane 1998, 60,000+ businesses, first DD provider with its own AFSL, now offers Ezidebit PayTo), Ezypay (1996, Australia's first DD company, 6,000+ merchants across 10 APAC countries), Debitsuccess/Xplor (largest full-service DD initiator in Australasia, 22M+ transactions/yr). GoCardless sizes the AU market at ~95 million direct-debit payments per month.
19. The PayTo natives and the cautionary tale: Zepto (Byron Bay, A$25M Series A) is Wpay/Woolworths' PayTo provider and put Red Energy live as the first major PayTo biller (Oct 2024); Monoova claims the majority of PayTo processing volume as of mid-2024. Till Payments went ~A$500M valuation → A$141M FY22 loss → US$36.9M sale to Nuvei (Jan 2024).
20. The room itself: judges are Ben Cull (Pinch co-founder, ex-Ezidebit engineer, public 'payments engineer', GitHub bjcull) and Carolyn Breeze (Scalare Partners CEO; ex-GoCardless ANZ MD, ex-Zepto CCO, ex-Braintree AU country manager, ex-PayPal/eBay). ## The 5 opinions worth holding (seniors have positions)

- OPINION 1 — PayTo doesn't kill direct debit; the realistic world is a decade of dual-rail. Defence: the 2030 end-date was formally removed (Dec 2025) precisely because existing debits aren't migrating — PayTo growth is new agreements, agreement value sits at ~0.1% of BECS DD value, ~5% of recurring volume converted, and mandate porting was pulled from the AP+ roadmap. Therefore every serious recurring-payments stack needs a BECS+PayTo abstraction layer with a migration story, and 'doesn't PayTo make this obsolete?' is the objection you WANT — it's your macro slide.
- OPINION 2 — PayTo's bottleneck is bank authorisation UX, not merchant demand or rail quality. Defence: the rail is already excellent where used (98.9% settlement, 4.94s median, +301% e-commerce growth in six months), yet one bank moved mandate authorisation conversion from 40% to 70% with nothing but a real-time push notification — and the three majors needed Governor letters just to go live. Implication: bet on orchestration, mandate-conversion tooling and retry intelligence, not on raw rail access, because the constraint is in the banking app, not the scheme.
- OPINION 3 — The dishonour lag is the product opportunity, not a defect to wait out. Defence: no positive confirmation plus 1–3 day return codes means BECS is structurally blind at debit time, and the rail will not fix this within any commercial horizon (see: end-date removed, RBA still consensus-hunting in 2026). So prediction beats reaction as an architecture: knowing which debits will bounce and moving them to payday — with consent — creates value the rail can never natively provide. This is Cadence's reason to exist, stated as an industry position rather than a feature.
- OPINION 4 — The 1 October 2026 reforms flip recurring-payment economics toward A2A, but only for operators who control failure costs. Defence: surcharging bans plus interchange cuts (~$910m/yr) make cards cheaper AND un-pass-through-able, so merchants will interrogate total cost per successful collection — where BECS/PayTo wins on headline price but loses if dishonour handling, retries and churn are unmanaged. Corollary: always price against the post-October blended card rate, and always quote unit economics as dishonour rate × cost per retry × false-positive dollars, never as raw transaction fees.
- OPINION 5 — Independent recurring-payments capability is now scarce, and the licensing perimeter is closing — so the winning posture is 'intelligence layer on licensed rails.' Defence: every independent asset was acquired within 24 months (Pinch, Ezypay, Nuapay, Pin/SecurePay, Payrix), proving incumbents buy capability rather than build it — that scarcity is the founder's leverage in any conversation. Simultaneously, PSRA Tranche 1 (exposure draft 12 March 2026) will likely capture payment-initiation software, so the defensible one-liner is: Cadence initiates nothing itself — it rides its PayFac's AFSL (Pinch's) and is architected to stay orchestration-side of the licensing line, with the perimeter monitored as it lands.

## Deploying the Cadence asset in a job conversation

THE ARTIFACT WALK-THROUGH — fixed order, whether the audience is Ben Cull at Demo Night or a hiring panel at Zepto/GoCardless/Airwallex. Principle: repo-first, outcome-first, jargon-last. The posture is equal-terms (partnership-or-senior-hire), earned by a working product on their rails, not by a resume.

STEP 1 — The loop, live or on film (60–90s). Open with the outcome, not the stack: "Dana just got this text — watch what she does." Failed-debit prediction → SMS at the right moment → payer replies "can't do Friday, Monday works" → parser catches the negation → Monday selected → debit lodged before the 16:00 exchange → SETTLED with the applicationFee line-item visible. This is the only theatrical moment; everything after is engineering. Say the one number pair: the $29.90 bounce fee versus $45 recovered.

STEP 2 — The ledger (the seniority signal). Open the actual schema/migration: append-only double-entry, reversals not updates, every state transition an immutable row. Say why: "you can't reconcile what you've overwritten." This is the top senior-payments interview signal and it lands hardest with Cull, a self-described payments engineer.

STEP 3 — Idempotency as a durable state machine. Show the (merchant_id, idempotency_key) → request-hash/response/status table and the 409-on-payload-mismatch path; explain at-least-once delivery + idempotent writes as the pairing. Two minutes here outperforms any ML discussion for establishing you've operated real money movement.

STEP 4 — Reconciliation. Show the three-way recon (internal ledger vs Pinch API state vs settlement file) and, ideally, a deliberately injected mismatch being caught. Narrate the BECS reality it exists for: no positive confirmation, dishonours trickling in over 1–3 days against a punch-card-era ABA file.

STEP 5 — The ML, framed honestly and in payments economics. Lead with the ablation as a two-bar picture: strip the pay-cycle structure and AUC collapses 0.913 → 0.501 — "a coin flip" — proving the signal IS payday timing, not leakage. Volunteer the synthetic-ledger caveat before being asked; disclosure-forward is what AU panels reward. All value quantified as dishonour dollars, cost per retry, and false-positive dollars — never precision/recall.

STEP 6 — The consent gate and compliance posture. Show the refusal path: the gate blocks any debit lacking mandate-consistent authorisation. Say the sticky line: "Prediction is the default; consent is the upgrade." Tie to E071 DDR evidence requirements and the 14-day variation rule — the compliance burden Cadence absorbs.

STEP 7 — The roadmap and the licensing one-liner. One slide: dual-rail abstraction — BECS collection today, PayTo Mandate Management Service integration next, ISO 20022-ready data model — because the realistic world is a decade of both rails. Licensing: "Cadence rides its PayFac's AFSL and stays orchestration-side of the Tranche 1 perimeter."

STEP 8 — The ask, calibrated to the room. Pinch/Fiserv: a 90-day pilot on one Pinch merchant cohort measured against the payday heuristic — positioned as accelerating Fiserv's Glassbox/Clover APAC embedded-payments strategy (they paid US$365M for exactly this platform; budget exists). Employer conversation: the repo plus this walk-through IS the interview — steer to a senior/staff scope discussion with comp anchored at market (A$180–260K senior, A$250–340K staff total comp), never below. Close every version with "the repo is public — go read it."
