## 2. What makes you read as senior in a payments interview

The gap between "built a model" and "shipped money movement" is the whole interview. Senior signals, in order:
- **Ledger instincts**: append-only double-entry, corrections as reversals not updates; you never mutate a posted amount.
- **Idempotency as a durable state machine**, not a cache: (merchant_id, idempotency_key) → request hash + response + status; same key/different payload → 409. Exactly-once effects = at-least-once delivery + idempotent writes.
- **Reconciliation religion**: "payment succeeded in our DB" is where juniors stop; seniors talk three-way recon against processor settlement files, webhook retries/dedup, dishonour and retry economics.
- **Rails fluency, AU-specific**: NPP/PayID/PayTo vs BECS direct entry vs cards. The killer current fact: AusPayNet **removed the June 2030 BECS end-date in December 2025**, and the RBA's March 2026 risk assessment says PayTo has "yet to demonstrate its maturity" — PayTo agreements were ~0.1% of BECS direct-debit value in 2025, growth is new agreements not migrations, and mandate porting was pulled from the AP+ roadmap. If you can discuss *why* migration stalled (mandate migration UX, corporate readiness, porting), you sound like an operator, not a candidate. Add the RBA's 2025 surcharging review and CDR/open banking for breadth.
- **Compliance instincts**: PCI DSS scope minimisation, AUSTRAC AML/CTF obligations, scam liability shifts — you flag them unprompted when designing.
- **Unit economics**: interchange vs scheme fees vs acquirer margin; cost-per-transaction of A2A vs cards; in fraud-ML, the false-positive cost in blocked-good-payment dollars, not F1 score.

## 3. Hackathons convert — if you work the room deliberately

Evidence: TopOpps hired **ten participants** from a single 2014 GlobalHack to build its whole dev team; Intellect Design Arena converted hackathon pipelines at ~61% (302 hires from 497 interviews); recruiters consistently rank hackathon wins as proof of pressure-tested shipping. Conversion mechanics for demo night: your targets are (1) the judges — Cull for the builder-to-builder conversation, Breeze for the network (she can open GoCardless, Zepto, and the Scalare portfolio in one intro); (2) The Founders Union organisers; (3) any Fiserv AU staff present; (4) the strongest other teams (future colleagues/referrals). The follow-up sequence that works: email/LinkedIn within **24–48 hours**, under 200 words, opening with one specific detail from the actual conversation; a relational ask (share the repo/demo link, offer something useful); second touch 5–7 days later with a new angle; the conversion goal is a 30-minute "advice" call — informational interviews surface roles that never get posted.
