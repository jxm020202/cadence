# The AU payments landscape map — who is who (2024–2026)

> Research briefing (sourced, July 2026). Part of the domain-mastery asset.

# The Senior Operator's Map — Australian Payments, 2024–2026

The single most important thing to internalise: **the Australian recurring-payments layer is being bought, not built.** In 24 months, nearly every independent name in the category changed hands — and the buyers are all global processors buying local capability. Walk into any room knowing the deal sheet cold.

## 1. Recurring / direct-debit — the consolidated core

- **Ezidebit (Global Payments).** Founded Brisbane 1998 by Michael Dempsey (a childcare-centre owner automating his own collections); acquired by NASDAQ-listed Global Payments in 2014. Claims 60,000+ businesses; first direct-debit provider licensed with its own AFSL; has launched Ezidebit PayTo. The vertical-SMB direct-debit incumbent (fitness, childcare, allied health).
- **Payrix / Worldpay.** Brisbane's IntegraPay was bought by US PayFac Payrix (Oct 2020, ~US$1.75B volume added), rebranded Payrix Australia (Sept 2021), then FIS bought Payrix (Feb 2022) — it now trades as **Worldpay for Platforms**. Critical wrinkle: **Global Payments announced (17 April 2025) it is acquiring Worldpay from GTCR/FIS for US$24.25B total value while divesting Issuer Solutions to FIS for US$13.5B** — meaning Ezidebit and Payrix Australia end up under one roof. Expect rationalisation, and refugee talent.
- **Ezypay (→ Xplor).** Sydney, founded 1996 by gym owner George Holman — Australia's first direct-debit company. 6,000+ merchants via partner/ISV channel, live in 10 APAC countries (Singapore its #2 market), $3B+ processed. **Xplor signed to acquire it 18 Sept 2025, closing Dec 2025**; CEO James Foster stays, folded into Xplor Pay.
- **Debitsuccess (Xplor).** NZ-born (1994; Australia 2002), the largest full-service direct-debit *initiator* in Australasia — 22M+ transactions a year, 10,000+ clients, strong fitness DNA (Clubware). With Ezypay, Xplor now owns two of the three big fitness-billing estates.
- **GoCardless AU.** London bank-payment specialist; its decisive local move was buying **Nuapay (Sentenial) from EML for €32.75M (A$54.1M), completed 2 Sept 2024** — creating a full-service collect-and-payout bank-payment provider spanning BECS direct debit and PayTo. GoCardless itself sizes the AU market at ~95M direct-debit payments per month.
- **Pinch (Fiserv).** Brisbane PayFac founded 2017; ~2,000 merchants AU/NZ; known for invoice auto-payment and its **Glassbox** PayFac-enablement platform. **Fiserv announced the acquisition in March 2025 — one week after announcing Clover's Australian launch.** Pinch is Fiserv's APAC embedded-payments/PayFac beachhead, not a standalone brand play. That's the frame for any pitch into it: you're pitching into Fiserv's Clover + ISV strategy, not a 30-person Brisbane startup.

## 2. Payment APIs / NPP / PayTo — the rails challengers

- **Zepto** (Byron Bay; founded as Split Payments): A$25M Series A (Mar 2022, AirTree/Decade). The marquee PayTo scalp-taker: named **Wpay's (Woolworths) PayTo provider**, put **Red Energy** live as the first major PayTo biller (Oct 2024), Spaceship (Aug 2025).
- **Monoova** (Sydney, backed by Moneytech): claims a **majority of all PayTo processing volume** as of mid-2024; Remitly PayTo deal (Apr 2025); Primer partnership; Global FinTech Awards **Company of the Year 2025**. The quiet volume leader.
- **Zai** (Melbourne): Assembly Payments (2013, one of Australia's oldest fintechs, Westpac-Reinventure-backed) merged with CurrencyFair in 2021 under SC Ventures (Standard Chartered) money. Strong in marketplaces/escrow (was REA/PEXA adjacent); has been strategically quiet 2024–25 — widely seen as having lost its early lead to Zepto/Monoova.
- **Azupay** (Sydney): PayID/PayTo enterprise-and-government specialist (ATO-adjacent use cases, airlines, universities); backed by Andrew Forrest's **Tattarang** plus a **A$10M Ellerston Capital** cheque; shipped passkey-enabled PayTo.
- **Hello Clever** (Sydney, 2021, Caroline Tran & Gavin Nguyen): A$4.5M seed (2022, Vectr); did the **first PayTo payment in Australia with the RBA**; consumer "buy-to-earn" plus merchant PayTo APIs. Smallest of the set.
- **Macro context you must recite correctly:** industry set a 2030 target to kill **BECS** (the direct-entry system every direct debit runs on). In **December 2025 AusPayNet removed the June 2030 end-date** because migration wasn't tracking — the RBA's risk assessment found PayTo growth was coming from *new* agreements, not migration of existing debits, and an RBA/Treasury/AP+/AusPayNet A2A roundtable (ACCC-authorised) is due to publish a roadmap in 2026. Translation: the BECS→PayTo replatforming is real but slower than the 2023 hype; direct debit remains a decade-long cash cow while every provider must build a dual-rail (BECS + PayTo) story.

## 3. SMB acquiring / POS

- **Tyro** (ASX:TYR, IPO Dec 2019): 76,000+ merchants, hospitality/health heartland; FY25 EBITDA A$61.6M (+10.6%), FCF A$19.6M. Survived the failed Potentia private-equity approach (2022–23); CEO **Jon Davey exited 5 Dec 2025**, **Nigel Lee (ex-Ingenico APAC MD)** starts 12 Jan 2026; buying SME money-platform **Thriday** (close Jan 2026).
- **Zeller** (Melbourne, 2020, ex-Square's Ben Pfisterer & Dominic Yap): valued **A$1B+ at its A$100M 2022 Series B**; now 85,000+ businesses; ~US$96M revenue (2025, third-party estimate); expanding from terminals into full SMB financial services. The fastest-growing local challenger.
- **Square/Block**: flat-rate incumbent-challenger since 2016; Banking Day shows Stripe and Square carving major share from the banks. The **Afterpay arc**: founded 2014 (Molnar/Eisen), bought by Square in the all-stock deal announced at ~US$29B / A$39B (closed Jan 2022) — still Australia's largest M&A; Afterpay is now Block's BNPL spine (Cash App Card integration in the US; **Cash App doesn't operate in Australia**). From **10 June 2025** BNPL is regulated in AU as low-cost credit (credit checks at sign-up).
- **Stripe AU**: ~A$551M Australian revenue in 2022 (+34%); supports BECS direct debit *and* PayTo natively — it's already a recurring-payments competitor, not just e-commerce.
- **Airwallex** (Melbourne-founded 2015): US$300M Series F at **US$6.2B** (May 2025) → Series G at US$8B (Dec 2025) → **US$320M Series H at US$11B (June 2026)**; US$720M annualised revenue (Mar 2025, +90%), 150k+ customers. Australia's biggest private fintech; hiring aggressively.
- **Till Payments — the cautionary tale**: founded 2012 (Shadi Haddad), valued ~A$500M, lost A$141M in FY22, cut 40% of staff, and was **sold to Nuvei for US$36.9M (Jan 2024)**. Cite it when asked why capital discipline matters in acquiring.
- **Fat Zebra** (Sydney gateway): the local consolidator — **Adatree** (CDR/open-banking, Feb 2024), **Pin Payments** (13,000+ SMBs, Dec 2024), **SecurePay from Australia Post** (35,000+ businesses, Apr 2025). Three deals in 14 months; now spans enterprise gateway → SMB.

## 4. Big rails / enterprise

- **Cuscal** (ASX:CCL): **listed 25 Nov 2024 at $2.50/share (~A$337M offer)** — the only non-Big-4 institution with the full ADI + payments capability set; one of 13 original NPP shareholders; connects ~22 fintechs (including Zepto-class clients) to the NPP; took control of open-banking platform **Basiq** (2023). If you touch NPP, you probably touch Cuscal.
- **EML Payments** (ASX:EML): the serial-trouble story — Irish regulator (CBI) remediation, then sold Sentenial/Nuapay to GoCardless (2024) to go net-cash; CEO **Ron Hynes hired 30 June 2024, terminated 21 Dec 2024** (shares −22%); "EML 2.0" refocus on gift cards/GPR under an executive chair. Shrinking.
- **Novatti** (ASX:NOV): ongoing strategic review — sold Emersion for A$500k (Sept 2025), closed EU/Malaysian entities, must dilute its 91% IBoA (bank) stake. Shrinking.
- **Adyen AU**: local acquiring licence since 2017; enterprise/omnichannel (retail majors). Small local team, high bar.
- **The banks**: **CBA** is the aggressor — PowerBoard (orchestration, built with Paydock) explicitly targets Stripe/Square customers. **Westpac** opened PayTo to billers (Apr 2024) via PayWay. **ANZ** is buying out **Worldline's 51% of ANZ Worldline for ~A$89M EV (announced 2026, completing 2H26)** amid Worldline's global "dirty payments" scandal — merchant acquiring back in-house. **NAB** runs its Transact/HICAPS estate. Banks are re-arming in merchant payments after a decade of retreat.

## 5. Who's hiring vs shrinking

**Growing:** Airwallex, Zeller, Fat Zebra, Monoova, Zepto, GoCardless AU (post-Nuapay), Fiserv AU (Clover launch + Pinch), Cuscal (post-IPO), Stripe AU, Xplor (integrating Ezypay). **Shrinking/absorbed:** EML, Novatti, Till (inside Nuvei), Zai (quiet), ANZ Worldline (in transition), Tyro (steady but in CEO reset).

## 6. Cadence: competitors, partners, and Plan B

**Real competitors** (same buyer, same job-to-be-done — recurring collections for Australian businesses): Ezidebit, Ezypay, Debitsuccess, GoCardless, Payrix/Worldpay, Stripe Billing/PayTo, and Pinch itself. **Natural partners/suppliers, not competitors:** Zepto, Monoova, Azupay, Cuscal (PayTo/NPP rails you'd build on), Fat Zebra (gateway), banks' PayTo programs. **Best alternative employers if Pinch/Fiserv doesn't bite**, ranked: (1) **GoCardless AU** — post-Nuapay it is building exactly a DD+PayTo collect/payout business and needs senior local operators; (2) **Xplor** — just bought Ezypay, owns Debitsuccess, integration leadership needed in Sydney/Auckland; (3) **Global Payments/Ezidebit** — the Worldpay integration puts Ezidebit + Payrix under one roof, a giant AU recurring estate to run; (4) **Zepto or Monoova** — PayTo-native scale-ups where recurring-mandate expertise is the product; (5) **Fat Zebra** — three acquisitions in 14 months, needs product/ops leaders to fuse Pin + SecurePay's subscription billing; (6) **Airwallex / Zeller / Stripe AU** — scale, capital, and open senior roles, though less recurring-specific.

## Applied to Cadence

This map sharpens the Cadence pitch in four concrete ways. (1) **The buyer's frame:** Fiserv didn't buy Pinch as a direct-debit shop — it bought Glassbox (PayFac enablement) one week after announcing Clover's Australian launch. So the pitch into Pinch/Fiserv should position Cadence (and the founder) as accelerating Fiserv's APAC embedded-payments/ISV strategy — recurring-revenue collection as a Clover/PayFac feature — not as 'another billing tool'. Name-check the integration reality they're living: 2,000 Pinch merchants being wired into a US$20B+ giant that is simultaneously watching its rival (Global Payments) swallow Worldpay and unify Ezidebit+Payrix. (2) **Scarcity argument:** every independent recurring-payments asset has been bought in 24 months (Ezypay→Xplor Dec 2025, Pin+SecurePay→Fat Zebra, Nuapay→GoCardless, Payrix→Worldpay→Global Payments, Pinch→Fiserv). Independent capability and the operators who built it are now scarce — that's the founder's leverage. (3) **The timing wedge:** BECS decommissioning slipped (June 2030 target removed Dec 2025) precisely because existing direct debits aren't migrating to PayTo — meaning every incumbent needs a dual-rail (BECS+PayTo) recurring stack and a migration story for the next 5+ years. If Cadence is that bridge, this is the macro slide of the pitch, cited to the RBA/AusPayNet record. (4) **Plan B, ranked:** if Pinch/Fiserv doesn't bite, the best-fit alternative employers are GoCardless AU (building exactly DD+PayTo collect/payout post-Nuapay, needs senior local operators), Xplor (integrating Ezypay + Debitsuccess — integration/GM roles), Global Payments/Ezidebit (running the combined Ezidebit+Payrix estate through the Worldpay merger), Zepto or Monoova (PayTo-native scale-ups where recurring-mandate expertise IS the product), and Fat Zebra (serial acquirer needing product/ops leadership to fuse Pin and SecurePay's subscription billing) — with Airwallex, Zeller and Stripe AU as high-growth wildcards. Avoid the shrinking side of the ledger (EML, Novatti, Zai, ANZ Worldline in transition).
