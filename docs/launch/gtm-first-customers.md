# Go-to-market & the first ten customers

> Launch-viability research (sourced, July 2026).

THE HEADLINE FINDING: Pinch's book is not a gym book — and that changes your sequencing, not your beachhead thesis.

1) What Pinch's ~4,000 merchants actually are. Pinch's own site and its Xero App Store presence (it is the top-rated Xero payment app) show a merchant base of service SMBs: accounting and bookkeeping firms, marketing agencies, IT/MSPs, payroll services, event companies, creative studios, education payments (StudentPay), trades and food businesses. Its named testimonials include Online Marketing Gurus, Digit Business, Evolution Cloud Accounting, Think Technology, Sway Events — zero gyms, zero childcare centres on the public roster. Pinch's explicit vertical pages target "service-based businesses" and "accounting & bookkeeping"; APAC Insider literally calls it "the payments platform built for accountants." Implication: your first 5 customers are almost certainly NOT gyms. They are Pinch merchants already running recurring BECS pre-approvals who already eat dishonours. Pinch has the dishonour data per merchant; the fastest possible GTM is asking Pinch (post-hackathon, with their blessing) for their top-20 merchants ranked by BECS failure count and offering a free 90-day pilot to five of them. Selling INTO the book beats bringing new merchants to Pinch by every measure: zero-CAC, warm intro, instant data access, and Pinch is motivated because every recovered debit is volume they clip at 1% + 30c (capped $5).

2) Quantifying Pinch's upside so you can pitch the co-sell. Pinch's published pricing: BECS 1% + 30c capped at $5, cards 1.95% + 30c, $5 failed-payment fee (BECS only), no monthly fees. A 300-member gym debiting ~$30/fortnight is ~$234k/yr of volume ≈ $4,500-4,700/yr of processing revenue to Pinch. Cadence recovering 60% of a 15% failure rate adds roughly $400-600/yr of incremental processing per merchant — small per merchant, but the real Pinch/Fiserv upside is (a) differentiation: GoCardless monetises exactly this with Success+ (recovers ~70% of failed payments, lifts success to 99.1%, bundled into its Advanced tier at 0.75% + 5c capped $5) and Pinch has no equivalent, so Cadence makes Pinch's rails competitive against GoCardless inside the Xero ecosystem; (b) merchant retention/acquisition — "the only Xero direct-debit rails that fix failed payments" is a co-marketable claim; (c) every NEW merchant Cadence brings is ~$4-5k/yr to Pinch. Precedent for platform+app co-selling exists at Pinch: they publicly co-announce integration partners (e.g. the Annature partnership got a GlobeNewswire release) and run a Partner Hub — ask for the same treatment.

3) The bookkeeper channel is real but it is a month-6 channel, not a week-1 channel. Xero App Store mechanics: you must pass ~9 certification checkpoints AND already have 5-10 customers actively using the app on Xero before you can list; Xero then takes a 15% referral revenue share on subscriptions originated through the App Store (other channels unshared). So the App Store cannot produce your first ten customers — it structurally requires them first. The practical bookkeeper motion is to piggyback Pinch's existing partner program (bookkeepers earn up to 5% of transaction fees for referrals; covered in Accountants Daily) rather than build your own: a bookkeeper who has already put 10 clients on Pinch is one conversation away from switching on Cadence for all of them. ICB Australia (bookkeeper body) runs 11-city summits and virtual EOFY events — one sponsorship (~$2-5k) puts you in front of hundreds of multi-client bookkeepers. Treat this as the scale channel after case studies exist.

4) The gym vertical is more locked-up than the thesis assumed — target independents only. Franchise chains are contractually closed: Anytime Fitness Australia licenses franchises ON THE CONDITION that franchisees use Debitsuccess (an ACCC-notified exclusive dealing arrangement, N99539); Fitness First signed Clubware + Debitsuccess network-wide. Xplor (Debitsuccess + Clubware), Ezidebit and PaySmart own franchise/chain billing end-to-end, and boutique franchise brands increasingly run Hapana (900+ clubs, $300M AUD/yr processed on Stripe). A solo founder cannot displace an HQ-mandated biller — 12-18 month enterprise cycles, and the software and billing are fused. The addressable slice is the ~2,100 independent operators and ~1,100 boutique studios (IBISWorld counts ~8,078 gym/fitness businesses total, industry consolidating), specifically those billing memberships as Xero repeating invoices rather than through gym-management software — yoga/pilates/PT studios, CrossFit boxes, small independents. Decision-maker there is the owner-operator; sales cycle 2-6 weeks; CAC is mostly founder time (cold email/DM + local visits), realistically $200-800 blended if you add any paid spend. This matches your solo-with-a-full-time-job constraint: you cannot run high-volume outbound, so gyms come via (a) Pinch's book, (b) fitness-specialist bookkeepers, (c) 2-3 lighthouse independents you personally close.

5) Pilot design that converts. Structure: 90 days, free, 3-5 merchants each with ≥150-200 active BECS mandates (you need ~100+ scheduled debits/fortnight for statistical signal). Baseline: pull 6 prior debit cycles from Pinch's ledger (dishonour rate, time-to-recovery, dollars written off) — this is your control, PLUS run the naive payday-heuristic (retry on the 15th/30th or next Thursday) as an explicit comparator arm on a random half of failures, so the case study proves the ML beats the obvious cheap alternative, not just "retrying beats not retrying" (GoCardless's 70%-recovery Success+ number is the bar to talk past). Success criteria agreed in writing upfront: ≥50% relative reduction in unrecovered dishonour dollars, ≥60% of soft failures recovered within 7 days, zero payer complaints, SMS consent-receipt rate ≥80%. In exchange for free: signed case-study rights (named, with numbers), a testimonial, and 2 referrals. Convert at day 90 to the 15% recovered-dollars applicationFee — pilots that hit the criteria have already seen the dollars, so conversion is a formality.

THE FIRST-10 SEQUENCE ($50k war chest, solo, nights-and-weekends):
- Customers 1-5 (months 0-3): via Pinch, from the hackathon relationship. Ask judges (Pinch co-founders) for the dishonour-ranked merchant list and a warm intro to five. These will be service SMBs — take them; the product is vertical-agnostic even if the marketing isn't. Cost: ~$0 CAC; spend goes to Twilio, infra, and a fixed-fee legal review of the SMS consent flow against BECS DDR/ePayments Code (~$8-10k).
- Customers 6-8 (months 3-6): independent gyms/studios on Xero. Source: one fitness-specialist bookkeeper (find via Xero advisor directory), plus direct outreach to boutique studios in your city. Budget ~$5k for an AUSactive/WNiF supplier listing or one event.
- Customers 9-10 (months 5-8): bookkeeper-referred, off the back of the published Pinch co-branded case study (ask Pinch for the same press treatment Annature got). Start Xero App Store certification now — you finally have the required customer count — so the listing goes live as your scale channel in months 8-10, accepting the 15% share only on store-originated deals.
- Explicitly defer: franchise HQs, gym-software partnerships (Hapana/Xplor are competitors-in-waiting, not channels), childcare and NFP verticals — all post-10.

Decision: GO on the GTM, but re-sequence — Pinch's book first (with Pinch's blessing formalised in writing: data access + co-marketing + non-compete comfort), gyms second as the marketing wedge and case-study vertical, bookkeepers third as the multiplier. The single biggest GTM risk is Pinch/Fiserv building this internally instead of blessing you — the hackathon IS the mitigation; get the partnership commitment while you have their attention.

## Key facts

- Pinch's public merchant base is service SMBs (agencies, accountants, IT, events, education payments) — no gyms, fitness, childcare or NFPs appear among its named customers or vertical pages
- Pinch pricing: BECS 1% + 30c capped at $5, cards 1.95% + 30c, $5 failed-payment fee on BECS, no monthly fees — so recovered volume earns Pinch ~1% and a new 300-member gym is worth ~$4.5k/yr in processing to Pinch
- Xero App Store listing requires passing ~9 certification checkpoints AND 5-10 active customers BEFORE listing, then takes a 15% revenue share on store-originated subscriptions — it cannot be a first-10 channel
- Pinch already runs a bookkeeper/accountant partner program paying up to 5% of transaction fees for referrals — piggyback it rather than building a channel
- Anytime Fitness Australia contractually mandates Debitsuccess for all franchisees (ACCC exclusive-dealing notification N99539); Fitness First is on Clubware + Debitsuccess — franchise gym chains are closed to a solo founder
- Hapana (900+ clubs, $300M AUD/yr) processes on Stripe; Xplor/Ezidebit/PaySmart own chain billing — the addressable gym slice is ~2,100 independents + ~1,100 boutique studios, specifically those billing via Xero
- GoCardless Success+ recovers ~70% of failed payments and lifts success rates to 99.1%, bundled in its Advanced tier (0.75% + 5c capped $5) — the proven benchmark Cadence must beat and the competitive gap in Pinch's stack
- IBISWorld counts ~8,078 gym/fitness businesses in Australia with the industry consolidating (~$2.8bn, declining) — vertical is real but shrinking, reinforcing recovery economics as the pitch
- Pinch co-announces integration partners with press releases (e.g. Annature via GlobeNewswire) and runs a Partner Hub — a template for a Cadence co-sell announcement
- A credible pilot needs merchants with 150-200+ active mandates, a 6-cycle historical baseline from Pinch's ledger, and an explicit payday-heuristic comparator arm, with case-study rights traded for the free period

## Go / no-go factors

- GO condition: Pinch formalises the partnership (dishonour-data access, warm intros into its book, co-marketing) — without Pinch's blessing the zero-CAC first-5 motion disappears and solo GTM is not viable alongside a full-time job
- Existential GTM risk: Pinch/Fiserv builds recovery internally (GoCardless already ships Success+ as a native feature) — the hackathon window is the one moment to lock in a written partnership instead of becoming their roadmap item
- The gym beachhead must be narrowed to Xero-billing independents/boutiques (~3,000 venues): franchise chains are contractually locked to Debitsuccess/Xplor (ACCC-notified exclusive dealing) and boutique franchises sit on Hapana/Stripe — if the wedge required franchises, it would be NO-GO
- First customers will be Pinch's existing service SMBs, not gyms — the product must be vertical-agnostic at launch or the fastest channel is wasted
- Xero App Store and bookkeeper channels only compound AFTER 5-10 customers and a published case study — plan cash and morale for a 6-9 month channel-building lag, and budget the 15% store rev-share into pricing
- Pilot credibility hinges on beating the payday-heuristic comparator and GoCardless's ~70% recovery benchmark with a 6-cycle baseline; a pilot that only shows 'retrying helps' will not convert or differentiate
