# Regulatory & structural path — licensing, DDR variation, SMS, privacy

> Launch-viability research (sourced, July 2026).

BOTTOM LINE: Cadence can launch today without its own AFSL if it holds its structure exactly: never touch funds, never charge the payer, act purely as the merchant's software agent on the merchant's own Pinch account, and keep payer SMS strictly factual. The two things that can actually kill the model are not licensing — they are (1) whether Pinch's standard Direct Debit Request Service Agreement (DDRSA) language covers third-party-initiated retries and payer-agreed date changes, and (2) the SMS Sender ID Register, which went fully live on 1 July 2026 and will label unregistered sender IDs "Unverified" — fatal for a product whose core mechanic is a payer trusting a text about their money.

1. LICENSING — where the line is today, and when PSRA bites.
Under current law the regulated financial product in this stack is the non-cash payment facility (Corporations Act; ASIC RG 185). That facility is issued by Pinch (a Fiserv company) and its sponsor bank; the merchant collects its own receivables through it. Cadence sits above both: it reads payment outcomes and instructs actions via the merchant's API credentials. The residual risk word is "arranging" — courts and ASIC read arranging-to-deal broadly (ASIC RG 36 territory) — but a pure technology agent that (a) never holds or controls funds, (b) is not the payer's counterparty, (c) doesn't issue or hawk the facility, and (d) gives no financial product advice to retail clients, is on the unlicensed-permissible side of the line as the market currently operates (this is how the entire Xero-app-ecosystem billing layer runs). The "likely-funded day" prediction is operational analytics delivered to the merchant, not financial product advice to a consumer. NCCP/credit licensing is avoided so long as the payer is never charged for the deferral — Cadence's 15%-of-recovered fee is merchant-side, so keep it there; introducing any payer-side late fee or deferral charge would change the analysis.
PSRA changes this, but slowly. Treasury's Tranche 1 exposure draft (released 12 March 2026, consultation closed 9 April 2026) replaces the NCPF concept with an activity-based list; "payment initiation services" — taking action that has the effect of initiating a non-cash funds transfer — is close to a literal description of Cadence's reschedule call, and "payment technology and enablement services" catches instruction-transmission layers. Two saving graces: the draft PTES definition excludes purely backend services with no direct payer/payee arrangement — but note Cadence's payer-facing SMS gives it a direct payer arrangement, so do NOT plan on that carve-out — and there is a low-value payment-services exemption for providers averaging under $8m/month in transfer value, which covers Cadence for years at gym-vertical scale. Timing: the bill is not yet before Parliament; commencement is 12 months after Royal Assent, with a further 6-month window for new entrants to apply. Realistically, no licensing obligation before ~2028, and the cheap path then is operating under Pinch/Fiserv's authorisation (representative/intermediary arrangement) rather than a solo AFSL. Separately, ASIC has already remade its low-value NCPF relief (Instrument 2026/167, extending relief to April 2031), signalling continuity for small facilitation layers in the interim.

2. THE DDR VARIATION QUESTION — this is the real legal mechanic.
BECS (AusPayNet Procedures, version E071, effective 1 Sep 2025) governs interbank processing; the payer-facing legal instrument is the DDR plus the DDRSA between the debit user (Pinch, on the merchant's behalf) and the payer. The enforcement teeth: any debit "not authorised by the DDR" is a Valid Claim — payer disputes are honoured effectively no-questions-asked (GoCardless documents banks honouring BECS disputes for up to 7 years; claims within 12 months compel a 5-business-day response from the debit user's bank). So an off-schedule debit the payer didn't agree to isn't a compliance footnote — it's an automatic refund plus a dispute strike against the merchant's (and Cadence's) standing with Pinch.
The variation rules split by who initiates. Merchant-initiated changes to drawing arrangements need ~14 days' written notice under standard DDRSA terms — "unless you agree to waive this" (NAB's DDR terms use exactly that formulation). Payer-initiated or payer-agreed variations can take effect immediately. This is why Cadence's SMS-confirm design is not UX garnish — it is the legal mechanism: the payer's reply converts a unilateral re-time (14-day notice, dispute-exposed) into an authorised variation (immediate, defensible). Customary retries after dishonour are likewise a matter of DDRSA disclosure, not BECS prohibition — market-standard agreements pre-authorise re-presentation (Stripe's BECS DDRSA, for example, contemplates retries and cancels arrangements after three consecutive dishonours). What the merchant's DDRSA must therefore contain for Cadence's mechanic: (a) express authority to re-present a dishonoured debit within a stated window; (b) express contemplation that debit dates may be varied by agreement via SMS/electronic confirmation; (c) dishonour-fee, dispute and cancellation terms. Critical dependency: merchants ride Pinch's DDR/DDRSA templates — so this is a Pinch conversation, not a Cadence document. First 90 days: obtain Pinch's standard DDRSA, map exactly what it already authorises, and if it's silent on payer-agreed retiming, negotiate template language (the hackathon puts the co-founders in the room). Store every consent receipt (exact payer words, timestamp, number, linked debit) as evidence-grade — it is the answer to a Valid Claim.

3. SMS — two regimes, one hard deadline already passed.
Spam Act 2003: a strictly factual payment-failure/reschedule message is a "designated commercial electronic message" — no consent or unsubscribe required, but accurate sender identification mandatory. ACMA is actively prosecuting businesses that stretch "factual" (Clayton Utz's enforcement review) — one promotional line ("upgrade your membership!") converts the message to commercial, requiring prior consent and a working unsubscribe. Keep Cadence SMS surgically factual, and capture SMS consent inside the DDR sign-up anyway (belt-and-braces, and it doubles as the variation-consent channel). Sender ID Register: registration opened 30 Nov 2025, deadline was 30 June 2026, and from 1 July 2026 unregistered alphanumeric sender IDs are labelled "Unverified" and grouped with scam messages on handsets. Sender IDs must be 2–11 ASCII characters, tied to the business, and cannot use restricted terms (verify/alert/banking etc.). Decision to make now: send as each gym's registered name (max payer trust, but requires per-merchant registration via an approved telco/EMSP) or one registered Cadence/platform ID. Either way, register before the first pilot SMS — an "Unverified" flag on a money-related text destroys the consent funnel.

4. PRIVACY — exempt now, don't build like it.
Cadence under $3m turnover sits inside the small-business exemption today — but (a) Tranche 2 reforms removing that exemption are expected 2026–27 with bipartisan support; (b) the automated-decision-making transparency obligation commencing 10 December 2026 requires privacy policies to disclose computer programs that make or substantially inform decisions significantly affecting individuals — an ML model deciding when someone's account gets debited is squarely that; (c) gym chains and Pinch/Fiserv will demand APP-grade handling contractually regardless. Practical posture: publish an APP-compliant privacy policy from day one, minimise data (payment outcomes and timing only — bank account numbers stay inside Pinch), treat consent receipts as personal information, have a Notifiable Data Breaches response plan, and document the model's role for the ADM disclosure.

5. COMMERCIAL LAYER WITH PINCH/FISERV.
Pinch runs a live partner program: Referral Partners (up to 5% of referred transaction fees, paid quarterly for two years) and Managing Partners — third parties who manage clients' Pinch accounts (built for bookkeepers/accountants, but structurally it is exactly Cadence's operating posture). The applicationFee rail is the native monetisation path. Ninety-day move: join the partner program, get written acknowledgment of the operating model (third-party API authority over merchant accounts + applicationFee on recovered payments), and use hackathon access to open the DDRSA-template and data-use conversation. What can wait: a formal ISV/platform agreement (liability allocation for disputed debits, PSRA-era authorisation under Pinch/Fiserv's licence) — needed before scale, not before pilot. Fiserv's AU referral/ISO partner machinery exists as the escalation path if Cadence outgrows Pinch's SMB program.

FIRST 90 DAYS (do): Pinch DDRSA audit + partner sign-up; sender ID registration; factual-only SMS templates; evidence-grade consent receipts; APP privacy policy + data minimisation; corporate structure with zero funds-handling and zero payer fees; note RG 96 (ACCC/ASIC debt-collection guideline) contact-frequency norms for payer messaging. CAN WAIT: AFSL/authorised-rep decision (monitor PSRA passage; 12-month + 6-month runway, sub-$8m/month exemption); full Privacy Act program (until Tranche 2, except ADM disclosure prep for Dec 2026); formal Fiserv-level agreements; AFCA membership (arrives with licensing).

## Key facts

- PSRA Tranche 1 exposure draft was released 12 March 2026 (consultation closed 9 April 2026); it replaces the non-cash payment facility concept with activity-based services — payment initiation, payment facilitation, and payment technology/enablement — all generally requiring an AFSL.
- PSRA commencement is 12 months after Royal Assent with a further 6-month application window for new entrants, and the bill is not yet law — realistically no licensing obligation for Cadence before ~2028.
- The draft includes a low-value payment-services exemption for providers averaging under $8m/month in transfer value, and the draft PTES definition excludes purely backend services — but Cadence's payer-facing SMS creates a direct payer arrangement, likely defeating that carve-out.
- Under current law, a software layer that never holds funds, never charges the payer, is not the payer's counterparty, and gives no financial product advice can operate on a merchant's own PayFac account without an AFSL; ASIC's remade low-value NCPF relief (Instrument 2026/167) runs to April 2031.
- BECS payer protection is effectively no-questions-asked: any debit 'not authorised by the DDR' is a Valid Claim; banks honour direct debit disputes for up to 7 years, and claims within 12 months compel a 5-business-day response from the debit user's bank.
- Merchant-initiated DDR variations need ~14 days' written notice 'unless you agree to waive this' (standard bank DDR terms), while payer-agreed variations can take effect immediately — Cadence's SMS confirmation is the legal mechanism that converts a re-timed debit into an authorised one.
- Retry after dishonour is governed by DDRSA disclosure, not BECS prohibition — market-standard agreements (e.g., Stripe's BECS DDRSA) pre-authorise re-presentation and cancel arrangements after 3 consecutive dishonours; merchants use Pinch's DDR templates, so Pinch must bless the mechanic.
- A strictly factual payment SMS is a 'designated commercial electronic message' under the Spam Act 2003 (no consent/unsubscribe needed, sender ID required), but ACMA actively prosecutes messages mischaracterised as factual — any promotional content triggers full consent + unsubscribe obligations.
- The ACMA SMS Sender ID Register is now mandatory: registration deadline was 30 June 2026, and from 1 July 2026 unregistered alphanumeric sender IDs are labelled 'Unverified' and grouped with scam messages on handsets.
- The Privacy Act $3m small-business exemption still stands, but Tranche 2 reforms removing it are expected 2026–27, and an automated-decision-making transparency obligation commences 10 December 2026 — directly applicable to an ML model that decides when consumers are debited.
- Pinch runs a live partner program: Referral Partners earn up to 5% of referred transaction fees for two years, and 'Managing Partners' manage clients' Pinch accounts — the structural template for Cadence's operating posture; Pinch is Fiserv-owned with Fiserv AU referral/ISO programs behind it.
- ACCC/ASIC RG 96 (debt collection guideline) applies to creditors and their agents contacting consumers about missed payments — contact hours, frequency and method norms apply to Cadence's payer SMS.

## Go / no-go factors

- GO-CONDITION (hard dependency): Pinch's standard DDR Service Agreement must authorise re-presentation after dishonour and payer-agreed debit-date variation via electronic confirmation — merchants use Pinch's templates, so if Pinch won't put the mechanic in its DDRSA, Cadence's core loop is non-compliant on its own rail. Resolve this in writing with Pinch during/immediately after the hackathon.
- GO (licensing): Cadence can operate today without an AFSL as a pure merchant-agent software layer — no funds handling, no payer-side fees, no financial product advice; PSRA will eventually capture it as a payment initiation service, but the 12-month post-Assent commencement + 6-month transition + sub-$8m/month low-value exemption gives a 2+ year runway, with the Pinch/Fiserv authorised-arrangement route as the cheap endgame.
- EXISTENTIAL RISK (disputes): BECS disputes are honoured no-questions-asked for years — a mis-timed debit without recorded payer consent is an automatic Valid Claim and a strike against merchant/Pinch standing. Evidence-grade consent receipts (exact words, timestamp, linked debit) are the product's legal foundation, not a feature.
- IMMEDIATE ACTION (already-live deadline): the ACMA Sender ID Register deadline passed 30 June 2026 — any unregistered alphanumeric sender ID now renders Cadence's consent SMS 'Unverified' and scam-adjacent on payer handsets, gutting the trust mechanic; register before the first pilot message and keep all SMS strictly factual under the Spam Act's DCEM exemption.
- WATCH (not blockers): Privacy Act Tranche 2 (small-business exemption removal, 2026–27) and the 10 Dec 2026 automated-decision-making disclosure obligation — build APP-grade privacy posture and model-disclosure language now because ML-decided debit timing is squarely in scope, but neither blocks a 2026 launch.
