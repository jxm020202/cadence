# Funding paths & strategic risk

> Launch-viability research (sourced, July 2026).

1) THE SCALARE/TFU PATH. The hackathon ("Pinch Me! I Want 50K", run by Pinch Payments + The Founders Union) is effectively Scalare Partners' deal-sourcing funnel. Scalare (ASX:SCP) is a micro-cap listed "investment + services" firm: historically ~8 startups/year at an average A$150k and max A$250k, with recent commitments ranging A$100k–$1.5m. It is not a deep-pocketed fund — Q3 FY26 revenue receipts were A$5.26m and underlying operating cash was roughly breakeven (–A$56k). Its model monetises services to founders (it acquired the InHouse Ventures capital-matching platform for A$1.3m; TFU is an AI services/perks hub with partners including Pinch, Westpac, MYOB, Atlassian). Expect any post-win offer to be a modest cheque possibly bundled with paid/equity services — read those terms with independent counsel. Crucially, the CEO-judge Carolyn Breeze is the ex-GM ANZ of GoCardless (she launched them in Australia; 185% revenue growth on her watch) and ex-CCO of Zepto. She will understand Cadence's Success+-analog instantly — and also know exactly what GoCardless already ships. Playing the dual role cleanly: during the competition, pitch strictly to the published criteria (innovation, technical execution, UX, commercial potential, effective use of Pinch tech) and never solicit investment from judges mid-event; the rules already state outstanding teams "may be invited to explore ongoing opportunities within the Pinch Payments and The Founders Union ecosystem" — let them open that door on 10 August. Incorporate a Pty Ltd and confirm Devpost IP terms (builders normally retain IP, but verify the fine print) before Demo Night so any conversation starts from owned, clean IP.

2) AU PRE-SEED LANDSCAPE FOR A SOLO FINTECH FOUNDER. Realistic options, best-fit first: Archangel Ventures (pre-seed specialist, fintech in scope, cheques A$100k–$500k, no accelerator lock-in) is the most compatible with keeping optionality. Startmate invests A$120k for 8% at a A$1.5m post-money cap over a 12-week program — solo founders accepted, but it expects full-time commitment. Antler Australia invests A$225k for 12% at A$1.875m post (with a A$68k program fee deducted) via an 8-week full-time residency (Feb & July intakes) — explicitly designed for solo/pre-team founders, but the residency is incompatible with holding the WeMoney job, and Antler pushes co-founder matching. Blackbird cuts the earliest cheques in ANZ ($25k pre-seed up to $5m seed) and would take a warm-intro meeting off a hackathon win; Square Peg is realistically a seed/Series-A conversation, not now. The solo-founder penalty is real but not disqualifying: solo founders were ~36% of new startups but only ~17% of those closing a VC round and ~14.7% of priced-round dollars (Carta 2025). A hackathon win judged by the very PayFac whose API you build on is exactly the kind of external validation that offsets it.

3) BOOTSTRAP MATH. Unit economics at 15%-of-recovered: a 500-member gym debiting ~A$30/fortnight collects ~A$390k/yr; at ~15% naive dishonour, ~A$58k/yr fails; curing 60–80% of that (the EFM 15%→3% case implies ~80%) recovers ~A$35–47k, of which Cadence keeps ~A$5–7k/gym/yr. Smaller gyms (~300 members) yield ~A$3–4k. So A$100k ARR ≈ 15–30 gyms; A$300k ARR (comfortable full-time replacement for an AU fintech salary, with costs) ≈ 45–90 gyms. Australia has roughly 4,200–7,800 fitness facilities, so the beachhead alone can carry the business to ~A$1m+ ARR before childcare/NFP expansion. The A$50k prize is tax-affected but buys ~6–9 months of frugal solo runway — or, better, stays banked while he remains at WeMoney and builds nights/weekends until ~A$5–8k MRR (15–25 gyms), then jumps with prize + revenue covering 12+ months. The transition trigger should be signed gyms, not the prize: prize-only full-time is premature. One hard gate first: his WeMoney employment contract almost certainly has IP-assignment and moonlighting clauses; WeMoney (consumer credit/financial-wellness) is adjacent-but-distinct from merchant payment recovery, but he must get a written IP carve-out and side-project consent BEFORE Demo Night — an ambiguous IP chain kills both the investment and the acquihire path.

4) COMPETITIVE RESPONSE — TIMELINES. The window is narrower than the pitch assumes on one axis and wider on the others. GoCardless Success+ intelligent retries are ALREADY live for BECS (AUD) — recovering ~70% of NSF failures — so "could GoCardless bring it to AU" is answered: it's here today, and they installed a new ANZ GM (Ian Boyd, Jul 2025) targeting >31% regional growth. The mitigations: Success+ requires merchants to move collections onto GoCardless rails, is an Advanced-plan/add-on feature, silently retries rather than rescheduling with payer consent, and GoCardless's ANZ push is oriented to SaaS merchants and PayTo — not Xero-native gym SMBs on a PayFac. Timeline to threaten Pinch's base specifically: 6–12 months if they targeted it, but channel overlap is low. Stripe is the least threatening: BECS Direct Debit is explicitly excluded from Smart Retries (its separate BECS retry is a dumb max-2-in-30-days), and AU DD is far down Stripe's global priority list — 12–24+ months. Ezypay already auto-rebills with >50% recovery and Xplor/Debitsuccess is the 1994-vintage incumbent (10,000+ businesses) — but both monetise failure via payer-punitive dishonour fees (~$14.80–$29.90), so matching Cadence's success-only, fee-free consent UX means cannibalising a revenue line: classic innovator's-dilemma drag, 12–24 months if ever. Fiserv building in-house is the real overhang and also the exit: they own Pinch (US$365m aggregate with Pinch's founders on earn-outs), and if Cadence proves recovered-revenue lift on Pinch data, Fiserv can staff it. Post-acquisition integration gravity (Pinch is being folded into the Clover/APAC agenda) suggests 18–24 months before an AU dishonour-ML feature gets internal roadmap attention — unless Cadence itself puts it on the agenda, which the hackathon does by design. Defensibility during the window: (a) proprietary dishonour-outcome training data accumulated on live Pinch merchants; (b) the consent-receipt UX — under BECS DDR/E071 rules a rescheduled debit needs payer authority, and stored consent SMS is a compliance asset silent-retry competitors don't have; (c) channel: native applicationFee pricing inside the PayFac the merchant already uses (no migration), which fee-dependent incumbents can't copy without self-harm.

5) LAUNCH VS JOIN — THE HONEST READ. The evidence tilts join-or-partner-with-Pinch/Fiserv over standalone venture: the product is single-platform-dependent (Pinch API), the founder is solo and part-time, the strongest analog (Success+) lives inside the rails provider rather than as an independent layer, and the buyer's corp-dev appetite is proven (Fiserv just paid $365m for the platform and has earn-out-incentivised founders who benefit from anything that grows processed-and-recovered volume). But the venture path isn't dead — success-priced recovery across Pinch + (later) other AU PayFacs/DD platforms is a real, bootstrappable business, and Archangel/Blackbird-scale cheques are plausibly available to a winner. He can run both simultaneously without poisoning either IF he: keeps IP in his own Pty Ltd with a clean chain (WeMoney carve-out signed); wins the hackathon on merit and lets Pinch/TFU make the first move; frames every conversation as "commercial partner on your rails" (revenue share, pilot, data access) rather than "hire me" — a partnership term sheet preserves both an acquisition path and a fundraising story; and defers any Scalare cheque until he knows whether Fiserv wants the asset, because a services-encumbered micro-cap on the cap table complicates a clean acqui. The one configuration that poisons both: signalling acquihire eagerness before proving revenue — it zeroes his negotiating leverage with Fiserv and tells VCs he's not building a company. Sequence: win → 90-day paid pilot on Pinch with 10–20 gyms → then choose between a Fiserv conversation from strength or a pre-seed round with live recovered-dollars data.

## Go / no-go factors

- GATE (must clear before Demo Night): written WeMoney IP carve-out + moonlighting consent, and a Pty Ltd holding the IP — without a clean IP chain, both the funding path and the Pinch/Fiserv path collapse
- GO signal: the hackathon's own rules invite winners into the Pinch/TFU ecosystem and the judges (Pinch co-founder, Scalare CEO) are exactly the channel + capital he needs — the cost of entry is one week of building
- NO-GO on the pure-novelty pitch: GoCardless Success+ already runs ML retry timing on BECS AUD — Cadence must be pitched as consent-based reschedule-to-funded-day on the merchant's existing PayFac (not 'smart retries for Australia'), or a judge who built GoCardless ANZ will discount it on sight
- Window assessment: 12–24 months of practical runway — GoCardless is channel-mismatched for Pinch's Xero-native SMB base, Stripe has explicitly excluded BECS from Smart Retries, and Ezypay/Xplor face dishonour-fee cannibalisation; the binding threat is Fiserv building in-house at ~18–24 months, which is equally the exit path
- Funding reality: Scalare cheque would be ~A$100–250k likely with services strings (defer until Fiserv intent is known); Antler/Startmate require quitting WeMoney; Archangel (A$100k–$500k, no residency) is the best-fit external capital; solo-founder discount is real (~17% of VC-round closers) but offset by a win + revenue
- Bootstrap viability: ~A$3–7k ARR per gym means ~15–30 gyms to A$100k ARR; the A$50k prize alone is NOT a quit-your-job event — the full-time trigger is ~15–25 signed gyms / ~A$5–8k MRR while salaried
- Dual-path discipline: run venture and partnership simultaneously ONLY as 'commercial partner on Pinch rails' framing; signalling acquihire eagerness before live recovered-dollar data zeroes leverage on both paths
