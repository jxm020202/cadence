# Market size, unit economics, pricing validation

> Launch-viability research (sourced, July 2026).

BOTTOM LINE: The economics work, but not the way the pitch implies. Per-site revenue is structurally small ($500–$4,000/yr), so Cadence is a channel-deal business from day one — one franchise HQ or one vertical-software integration is worth more than a year of direct SMB sales. Gross margin is ~95%, break-even is trivial (~15–30 merchants), and 15%-of-recovered survives a 50% haircut on the recovery uplift. The number that decides everything is not the 15% naive decline rate — it's the incremental uplift over the >50% auto-rebill recovery that Ezypay/Ezidebit-class incumbents already deliver.

1) TAM/SAM/SOM. Top-down: BECS processed ~3.5B payments worth A$17.4T in 2024, but that includes direct credits and high-value transfers, so build bottom-up by vertical. Fitness: IBISWorld puts AU gyms at ~6,583 sites and $3.7bn revenue (2025), collected overwhelmingly by direct debit. Assume 90% of revenue is DD-collected (~$3.3bn) and 5–8% of debit events fail first attempt (EFM ran ~15% on manual billing; well-run electronic billing lands 3–5%): first-attempt failed value = $165–265M/yr. The recovery-uplift pool — value a better system recovers beyond the status quo — is the honest TAM. If incumbents recover ~50% (Ezypay's stated ">50%") and Cadence reaches 65–75% (GoCardless Success+ hits ~70% vs a 38% naive baseline), the incremental pool is 15–25 points of $165–265M ≈ $25–65M/yr recovered value in fitness; at a 15% fee that's a $4–10M/yr revenue ceiling in the beachhead vertical alone. Childcare is the bigger pool: 15,300 CCS-approved services, 1.45M children, 1.02M families; at ~$144/day average long-day-care fees, gross fees run ~$25B+/yr, of which family gap fees (post-CCS out-of-pocket) are plausibly $8–12B/yr — and since July 2023 gap fees MUST be collected electronically under Family Assistance Law, with DD the dominant rail. Even at a modest 2–4% failure rate that's $160–480M/yr of failed childcare debits; incremental-recovery pool $25–120M; fee ceiling $4–18M/yr. NFP regular giving is small (DD failure ~1% vs ~13% for cards; 16% of donations by DD) — a $1–4M fee ceiling, a third vertical not a driver. SAM across the three verticals: roughly $10–30M/yr of realistically winnable fee revenue. SOM (3 yr, Pinch-anchored): Pinch had ~2,000 merchants at the April 2025 Fiserv acquisition (~4,000 claimed now); if 10–20% are recurring-billing SMBs and Cadence converts a third plus 1–2 channel deals, $0.5–2M ARR is the credible range. This is a strong solo-founder/lifestyle-to-seed business, not standalone venture scale — venture scale requires Fiserv distribution (Clover launched in AU the week Pinch was acquired) or geographic expansion.

2) Pricing validation. The market prices recovery three ways: (a) GoCardless Success+ charges an additional 0.5–1% per recovered payment and lifts recovery from a 38% baseline to ~70%; (b) Butter Payments takes a negotiated rev-share of recovered invoice value, pay-only-on-success; Gravy charges a flat fee; RecoverStripe-style tools charge 8%/6%/3% tiers of recovered dollars; (c) collections agencies take 20–30%. Forrester (cited by GoCardless) puts the true cost of manually recovering a failed payment at 11–15% of its value — Cadence's 15% sits exactly at the top of that band, i.e. it prices at "what recovery costs you today," pure upside-share, zero fee on healthy volume. Defensible for SMBs, with two caveats. First, GoCardless proves an enterprise player can deliver the same outcome at 50–100bps — 15–30x cheaper — so 15% only holds while the buyer is an SMB with no alternative and the absolute dollars stay small ($5.25 on a $35 fortnightly debit; a gym owner will not procurement-negotiate that). Second, charge 15% on payments Cadence actually recovers (attributable saves), not on everything that eventually clears — inflated attribution is the fastest way to churn merchants. The incumbents' effective "pricing" is the strongest sales weapon: Ezidebit charges the PAYER $21.90–$29.90 per dishonour (85% of a $35 debit; a Melbourne gym owner publicly attacked these fees, and Australian precedent requires dishonour fees to reflect actual failure cost) and Ezypay charges failed-payment fees; even Pinch charges the merchant $5 per declined DD. Incumbents monetise failure; Cadence monetises success and makes the payer's punitive fee disappear. That is a clean wedge and a genuine incentive realignment.

3) Solo-founder P&L. COGS per recovery: 2–3 SMS at ~A$0.08 (Twilio AU, ~US$0.0515/segment) ≈ $0.25; inference is batch-scored tabular ML ≈ nil; Pinch's normal 1%+30c (capped $5) BECS fee on the re-scheduled debit is the merchant's cost, not Cadence's. On a $35 recovery: $5.25 revenue, ~$0.30 COGS → ~94% gross margin. Per-merchant math: an average gym (~$560k revenue, ~$500k DD-collected) with 8% first-attempt failure has $40k/yr failed; lifting recovery from 50% to 70% recovers an incremental $8k, plus attribution credit on managed retries plausibly doubles billable saves → $1,200–$4,000/yr per mid-size gym at 15%; small studios $500–$1,500. Year 1 (10–50 direct merchants): $15k–$150k revenue, midpoint ~$45k exiting run-rate. Fixed costs (hosting, SMS, insurance, accounting, no salary) ~$15–30k/yr → break-even at roughly 15–30 merchants. Year 2 (200–500 sites via 1–2 channel deals): $200k–$1M ARR. Full-time trigger: replacing a WeMoney salary needs ~$250k ARR ≈ 150–250 sites ≈ exactly one mid-size franchise deal — do not quit before a channel deal is signed, and note the founder can run this to ~100 sites nights-and-weekends because the product is autonomous by design.

4) Channel-deal economics. Anytime Fitness AU alone is ~580–600 clubs (730k members; targeting 800 clubs by 2030): at $1–2k/site that single HQ relationship is $580k–$1.2M ARR — more than the entire year-2 plan. Jetts (~126 AU clubs) or Plus Fitness (~200) are $130–400k each. In childcare, one integration with a CCMS platform (Xplor Education et al.) exposes thousands of the 15,300 CCS services; 500 services at ~$1k = $500k ARR. The math says: one HQ deal ≈ 200–500 direct sales cycles. Hackathon judges (Pinch co-founders, Fiserv SMB VP) are themselves the mega-channel — Fiserv/Clover distribution would make every per-site number above look quaint.

5) Sensitivity. If real uplift is half the synthetic number (10 points over incumbent baseline instead of 20), per-gym revenue falls to ~$600–$2,000/yr. At 94% gross margin the model still works — nothing about COGS breaks — but the full-time ARR threshold doubles to ~300–500 sites, making a channel deal mandatory rather than accelerating. The scarier sensitivity is the failure-rate baseline: the "15% naive decline" belongs to manually-billed gyms (EFM pre-Ezypay); gyms already on Ezypay/Ezidebit run 3–5% with auto-rebill included free-ish. If Cadence's addressable failure pool is 4% and uplift is 10 points, a $500k gym yields ~$300–600/yr — direct sales dead, channel-only, and pricing pressure to justify even 15%. Kill-criterion to establish before committing: on real Pinch merchant data, Cadence must show ≥10 points of attributable recovery uplift over the merchant's existing retry outcome, measured on ≥3 months of debits. Below that, the product is a feature Pinch/Fiserv builds internally, not a company.

## Key facts

- BECS processed ~3.5 billion payments worth A$17.4 trillion in 2024 (includes direct credits; consumer recurring DDs are a subset — size verticals bottom-up)
- AU fitness: ~6,583 gyms, $3.7bn industry revenue (IBISWorld 2025), overwhelmingly collected by direct debit; Anytime Fitness alone has ~580-600 AU clubs and 730k members
- EFM Health Clubs (Ezypay case study): decline rate fell from ~15% (manual billing) to ~3% after moving to electronic DDR — i.e. the 15% figure is a MANUAL-billing artefact; electronically-billed gyms already run 3-5%
- Ezypay states a >50% success rate collecting failed payments via auto-rebill — this, not a 38% naive baseline, is the incumbent bar Cadence must beat
- GoCardless Success+: recovers ~70% of failed payments vs 38% baseline, priced at an additional 0.5-1% per recovered payment (15-30x cheaper than Cadence's 15%)
- Forrester (via GoCardless): manual recovery costs 11-15% of the failed payment's value — Cadence's 15%-of-recovered prices at the top of the manual-cost band
- Ezidebit charges the PAYER $21.90-$29.90 per dishonour (85% of a $35 fortnightly gym debit); Pinch charges merchants $5 per declined DD — incumbents monetise failure, Cadence monetises success
- Pinch fee schedule: BECS DD 1%+30c capped at $5, no monthly fees; ~2,000 merchants at the April 2025 Fiserv acquisition
- Childcare: 15,300 CCS-approved services, 1.45M children, 1.02M families; avg long-day-care fee ~$144/day; gap fees MUST be collected electronically since July 2023 (Family Assistance Law) — gap-fee pool plausibly $8-12B/yr
- NFP: DD failure rates ~1% vs ~13% for recurring cards; only 16% of AU donations by DD — small third vertical, not a driver
- Unit economics: ~$5.25 revenue on a $35 recovery vs ~$0.30 COGS (2-3 Twilio SMS at ~A$0.08 + nil compute) = ~94% gross margin
- Per-merchant revenue: $500-$4,000/yr per gym; break-even ~15-30 merchants; founder full-time (~$250k ARR) needs ~150-250 sites = one franchise HQ deal; Anytime Fitness HQ alone = $580k-$1.2M ARR at $1-2k/site
- Fee-revenue ceilings: fitness $4-10M/yr, childcare $4-18M/yr, NFP $1-4M/yr — SAM ~$10-30M/yr; 3-yr SOM $0.5-2M ARR; venture scale requires Fiserv/Clover distribution or geo expansion
- Half-uplift sensitivity: model survives (94% GM), but full-time threshold doubles to 300-500 sites — channel deals become mandatory, not optional

## Go / no-go factors

- GO only as a channel business: per-site revenue is structurally $500-$4,000/yr, so the plan must be anchored on franchise-HQ and vertical-SaaS deals (one Anytime Fitness-scale deal = $580k-$1.2M ARR); direct SMB sales alone cannot reach founder full-time (~$250k ARR)
- Decisive metric before committing: >=10 points of ATTRIBUTABLE recovery uplift over the merchant's existing auto-rebill outcome (Ezypay-class incumbents already recover >50%), measured on >=3 months of real Pinch debit data — below that, Cadence is a Pinch/Fiserv feature, not a company
- The '15% naive decline' beachhead stat is a manual-billing artefact (EFM pre-Ezypay); electronically-billed gyms run 3-5% failure — size the pool on 4-8% first-attempt failure, not 15%, or the revenue model is 2-3x overstated
- 15%-of-recovered is defensible (Forrester 11-15% manual cost, collections 20-30%, RecoverStripe 8%) but GoCardless Success+ delivers the same outcome at 0.5-1% — pricing power exists only in the SMB segment and only on clearly attributed saves; charge on Cadence-attributed recoveries, not all cleared payments
- Unit economics are robust: ~94% gross margin, COGS ~$0.30/recovery, break-even at ~15-30 merchants — the business survives a 50% haircut on uplift; what it cannot survive is a direct-sales-only go-to-market
- Ceiling check: SAM ~$10-30M/yr fee revenue across fitness+childcare+NFP — a great solo/seed business but venture scale requires the Fiserv/Clover distribution path (the hackathon judges ARE that path) or expansion beyond AU verticals
- Childcare is the larger and legally-tailwinded pool ($8-12B/yr gap fees, electronic collection mandated since Jul 2023, 15,300 services) — fitness is the wedge, childcare is the expansion that justifies the company
